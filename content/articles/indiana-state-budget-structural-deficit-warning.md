Indiana's nonpartisan legislative budget analysts warned lawmakers this month that the state faces a widening structural deficit over the next several fiscal years, driven primarily by rising Medicaid enrollment costs and continued implementation of the state's school funding formula changes.

The analysis projects the gap between projected state revenue and mandated spending will continue growing unless lawmakers either raise new revenue or scale back planned spending commitments, a dynamic budget officials described as a longer-term structural issue rather than a temporary shortfall tied to any single economic downturn.

"This isn't a one-year problem that a good economy fixes on its own," said the state's chief budget analyst in testimony to lawmakers. "The spending commitments already built into current law are growing faster than the revenue structure we currently have can sustainably support."

The report specifically flagged rising costs associated with the state's school funding formula, which commits the state to increased per-pupil funding over the coming years, alongside enrollment growth in the state's Medicaid program that has outpaced earlier projections. Analysts said addressing the gap would likely require some combination of tax changes and spending adjustments rather than relying on one option alone.

State lawmakers from both parties acknowledged the seriousness of the projections during budget hearings, though they differed sharply on preferred solutions, with some pushing for new revenue sources and others calling for slowing the pace of the school funding formula's phase-in. The governor's office said it would present a plan addressing the projected gap as part of the upcoming budget proposal.
