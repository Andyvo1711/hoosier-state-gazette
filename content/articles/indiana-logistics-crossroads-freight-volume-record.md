Freight volume moving through Indiana's interstate corridors reached a record level last year, according to figures released by the state's department of transportation, reinforcing the state's long-standing position as the "Crossroads of America" for national freight movement.

Transportation officials said truck freight volume climbed significantly compared with the prior full year, driven by continued growth in e-commerce distribution centers concentrated around Indianapolis and along the I-65 and I-70 corridors, along with sustained strength in Indiana's automotive and steel manufacturing exports moving to other states.

"We sit at the intersection of more major interstates than almost any other state in the country, and that geography keeps translating into new distribution center announcements every year," said the department's chief logistics economist. "Companies don't choose Indiana for the scenery, they choose it because you can reach a huge share of the U.S. population within a day's drive."

The state's distribution center construction pipeline remained active throughout the year, with several new large-scale fulfillment centers breaking ground near Indianapolis and Plainfield, an area that has become one of the densest concentrations of warehouse space in the Midwest. Officials said continued investment in highway maintenance and expansion has helped the state maintain its competitive position against neighboring logistics hubs in Ohio and Illinois.

The Indiana Department of Transportation said the record freight volume supports tens of thousands of direct and indirect jobs tied to logistics operations across the state, from truck drivers to warehouse workers. State economic development officials have continued to emphasize the state's central geography as a core pitch to companies considering new distribution facilities.
