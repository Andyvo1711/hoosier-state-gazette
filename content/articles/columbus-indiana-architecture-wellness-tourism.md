Spas in Columbus, Indiana, a city internationally known for its concentration of modernist architecture, have begun bundling treatment packages with the city's popular architecture walking tours, an effort to capture visitors who already travel to the city for its buildings but previously had little reason to book an overnight stay.

The packages typically combine a guided architecture tour with a spa treatment and a hotel stay, positioning wellness services as a complementary add-on for architecture tourists rather than the primary draw, a strategy local business owners say has helped convert day-trip visitors into overnight guests who spend more locally.

"People fly in from all over the world just to see our buildings, and for a long time we didn't give them much reason to stay past dinner," said the owner of a Columbus day spa that developed a package with the local visitors bureau. "Pairing a treatment with the tour gives them a reason to book a room instead of driving back to Indianapolis the same night."

The city's visitors bureau has supported the packages as part of a broader effort to extend the average length of visitor stays, noting that architecture tourists have historically been a strong draw for day trips but a weaker one for overnight hospitality spending compared with other tourism categories. Spa owners said early package bookings have skewed toward out-of-state visitors making a special trip specifically for the architecture.

Bartholomew County tourism officials said they view the spa packages as a low-cost way to capture additional visitor spending without requiring new infrastructure investment, since the city's architectural draw and hospitality capacity already existed independently. Spa owners said they plan to expand package offerings if the initial partnership proves successful.
