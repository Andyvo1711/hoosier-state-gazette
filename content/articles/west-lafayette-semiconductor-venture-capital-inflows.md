Venture capital investment in Indiana's growing semiconductor research sector, concentrated around Purdue University's West Lafayette campus, reached its highest level in five years last quarter, according to data compiled by a regional technology trade group, driven largely by continued investor interest in chip design and packaging startups.

The corridor, anchored by Purdue's semiconductor degrees program and its designation as a federal hub for microelectronics research, has developed a growing concentration of companies spun out of university research, and funding data shows investment increasingly concentrated in companies moving chip designs toward commercial manufacturing partnerships.

"What's different about this corridor compared with somewhere like Austin or Silicon Valley is how much of the early research traces directly back to a single university program," said the trade group's chief economist, who compiled the funding report. "Investors have started to recognize that pipeline as a genuine competitive advantage rather than treating Indiana as a secondary chip hub."

The report found that Indiana-based semiconductor companies raised significantly more in funding rounds compared with the prior year, even as overall national venture funding for early-stage hardware deals remained relatively flat. Several corridor companies completed funding rounds exceeding $50 million during the period, a threshold Indiana companies rarely reached as recently as five years ago.

State economic development officials have pointed to the funding data as validation of continued public investment in semiconductor incentives, including tax credits for chip companies that locate or expand within the state. Industry representatives said the corridor's continued growth will depend partly on whether Indiana can keep pace with laboratory and cleanroom space construction to accommodate expanding companies.
