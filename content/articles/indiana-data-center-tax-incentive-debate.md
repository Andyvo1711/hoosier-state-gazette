Indiana lawmakers are debating an expansion of tax incentives aimed at attracting large-scale data center development to the state, an effort to build on several recent large data center announcements in rural counties driven by growing demand for cloud computing and artificial intelligence infrastructure.

The proposed legislation would extend and expand existing sales tax exemptions on data center equipment purchases, a policy the state has already used to attract several major projects, in hopes of capturing more of the enormous capital investment flowing into data center construction nationally.

"We've already landed some significant projects, and this bill is about making sure we stay competitive as more of these projects get sited over the next several years," said a state senator sponsoring the incentive legislation. "Other states are watching what we've done and trying to match it."

Critics of the proposal, including some environmental advocates and fiscal watchdog groups, have raised concerns about the significant electricity and water consumption associated with large data centers, along with questions about whether the relatively modest permanent job creation from data center operations justifies the scale of tax exemptions typically required to attract such projects.

Data center industry representatives testified that Indiana's existing tax structure and available rural land near major transmission lines have made the state increasingly competitive for large-scale data center site selection. Lawmakers said they expect a vote on the incentive package before the current legislative session concludes, though the bill's future remains uncertain given the mixed reception from environmental and fiscal policy groups.
